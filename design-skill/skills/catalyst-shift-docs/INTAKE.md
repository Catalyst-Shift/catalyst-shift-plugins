# Intake — what to ask when context is thin

When a teammate invokes this skill with a one-line ask ("make a proposal for Acme"), you need more before you can write a credible doc. Ask in **one bundled message**, not piecemeal.

These questions are about the client. What we offer them, how it's priced and who on our side leads come from canon and the teammate (see **Read canon first** in `SKILL.md`).

## Universal questions (always ask if missing)

1. **Company** — name, and one line on what they do.
2. **Size** — headcount, roughly, and how established the company is.
3. **Leadership** — who runs it, who we're writing to (name, role), and what they care about most.
4. **Where it runs slower than it should** — in their words. What made them take the conversation now?
5. **Systems** — the tools and platforms the work touches, and any AI they already pay for.
6. **Services** — which of the six services fit (see `BRAND.md`). Check the engagement shape against canon.
7. **Stage** — first conversation, after the discovery session, after a signed agreement, or in delivery.
8. **Obligations** — any regulation or contract terms that limit what we can touch or say. If the client works under CMMC or handles CUI: confirm the work stays inside their boundary, and that no line implies we make them compliant or hold their CUI.
9. **Format preference** — HTML/PDF, PPTX, DOCX, Markdown. Default per the table in `SKILL.md`.

## Doc-specific add-ons

### Proposal
- The **price for this scope** and the **Stay monthly**. You don't invent or estimate either.
- The weeks for **Read, Build and Land** on this deal, and when Stay starts.
- Specific outcomes to put **in writing** — the deliverables and the outcomes we'll measure, with dates. *(Not "keep working at no cost.")*
- Has earlier work been done, and is it **credited** against this? Only write "credited" if the teammate says so.
- Is the deal through a partner or program with its own terms? If so, the teammate supplies the terms.

### SOW (statement of work)
- Has the proposal been accepted? If not, write the proposal first.
- Final price, the Stay monthly, payment schedule, term.
- Final deliverables and measured outcomes **in writing** — specific and measurable.
- Kickoff date and the weeks for each phase.
- Who is the CS lead and signer? Ask the teammate; don't assume.

### Discovery report
- When was the discovery session? Pull from transcript or notes.
- Where does the company run slower than it should? Use their words.
- Which services fit, and what's the recommended first engagement?
- Did we commit to a proposal by a specific date?

### Sales deck
- Audience — the CEO alone, the leadership team, a board, a partner?
- Length — 8 / 10 / 12 slides?
- Which services lead — check them against canon.
- Any real proof the client has approved in writing? If none, delete the proof slide; never invent one, never name ScalePad.

### Client deliverable
- Which engagement and which deliverable is this (e.g. a build handoff, an operating guide, an AI adoption plan)?
- What's the actual content the teammate has? (The skill formats and brands; it doesn't invent the work product.)
- Internal review or client-final?

### Case study
- **Has the engagement produced a measured outcome against the SOW?** If not, do not write a case study — propose a short update instead.
- Written consent to name the client and quote anyone? If not, write it anonymized (industry, size, region). Partners are never named before they sign and approve.
- Specific measures, pulled from the SOW.

### One-pager
- What single thing is this for — one service, a few, or the firm?
- Where will it be used — email attachment, leave-behind, a partner share, an event?

### Internal doc
- What kind — meeting notes, retro, decision memo, planning doc?
- Audience — the co-founders, or extended (advisor, partner)?
- Action items vs reference? If it records a decision, apply `decision-governance` (proposed vs decided).

## When the teammate gives you a transcript

Read it once. Pull:
- Decision-maker name + role + what they kept coming back to
- Where they said the company runs slower than it should (quote it back in the doc, with consent)
- Size, leadership and the systems mentioned — even partial
- Obligations mentioned (CMMC, DFARS, ITAR, HIPAA, client confidentiality)
- Any explicit asks ("send us a proposal by Friday")
- Any deal-breakers (budget, timeline, must-haves, data-handling concerns)

Then ask only what's *not* in the transcript.

## When the teammate gives you a prior doc to remix

Read it. Note what's reusable (structure, voice, sections) and what needs to change (client, scope, price, dates, outcomes). If its positioning, services, team or pricing don't match the site and current canon, flag what's out of date and rewrite it; take any price from the teammate, never from the old doc. Confirm the new client and scope, then write.
