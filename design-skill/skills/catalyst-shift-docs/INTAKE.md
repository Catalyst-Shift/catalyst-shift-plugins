# Intake — what to ask when context is thin

When a teammate invokes this skill with a one-line ask ("make a proposal for Acme"), you need more before you can write a credible doc. Ask in **one bundled message**, not piecemeal.

These questions are about the client. What we offer them, how it's priced and who on our side leads come from canon and the teammate (see **Read canon first** in `SKILL.md`).

## Universal questions (always ask if missing)

1. **Client / company name** — and one line on what they do.
2. **Industry** — what they do and any regime they work under. For a defense contractor: prime or sub, and whether they're on commercial Microsoft 365, migrating, or on GCC / GCC High. For an MSP: say so. The industry decides the language and which client-copy limits come into play.
3. **Size** — headcount and Microsoft 365 seats; for an MSP, revenue band and managed-client count.
4. **Systems** — their Microsoft 365 tenant (commercial or GCC High), licensing (Copilot or not), and the line-of-business systems the work touches. For an MSP: CRM + PSA + RMM.
5. **Decision-maker** — name, role, what they care about most.
6. **Offer** — what we're proposing. Check it against the current offers in canon.
7. **Stage** — first conversation, post-assessment, post-signed agreement, or delivery.
8. **Format preference** — HTML/PDF, PPTX, DOCX, Markdown. Default per the table in `SKILL.md`.

## Doc-specific add-ons

### Proposal
- The **price for this scope** and any **recurring line** (the templates carry a care-contract monthly). You don't invent or estimate either.
- Has an assessment been run, and is it **credited** against this work? Only write "credited" if the teammate says so.
- Specific outcomes to put **in writing** — the deliverables and the outcomes we'll measure, with dates. *(Not "keep working at no cost.")*
- Which stops on the spine (Connect · Normalize · Synthesize · Act · Keep) this engagement covers, and what keeps running after handoff.
- Constraints — compliance regime (CMMC level, DFARS, ITAR, HIPAA, client confidentiality), data residency, who owns tenant access. **For a defense contractor:** confirm the work stays inside their boundary and that no line implies we make them compliant or hold their CUI.
- Is the deal through a partner or program with its own terms? If so, the teammate supplies the terms.

### SOW (statement of work)
- Has the proposal been accepted? If not, write the proposal first.
- Final price, any recurring line, payment schedule, term.
- Final deliverables and measured outcomes **in writing** — specific and measurable.
- Start date and kickoff.
- Who is the CS lead and signer? Ask the teammate; don't assume.

### Discovery report / assessment findings
- When was the conversation or assessment? Pull from transcript or notes.
- What problem(s) did they surface? Use their words.
- Which offer and which stops on the spine fit, and what's the recommended first engagement?
- Did we commit to a proposal by a specific date?

### Sales deck
- Audience — single owner, leadership team, a group of prospective clients, a partner, an MSP?
- Length — 8 / 10 / 12 slides?
- Lead offer — check it against canon.
- Any real proof points to feature? If none, lean on the spine, the accountability promise, and honest build state — never fabricate, never name ScalePad.

### Client deliverable
- Which offer and deliverable is this (e.g. a readiness report, an evidence binder, a system of record handoff)?
- What's the actual content the teammate has? (The skill formats and brands; it doesn't invent the work product.)
- Internal review or client-final?

### Case study
- **Has the engagement produced a measured outcome against the SOW?** If not, do not write a case study — propose a short update or "principles in practice" doc instead.
- Client approval to use their name? If not, write it anonymized (industry + size + region). Partners are never named before they sign and approve.
- Specific metrics, pulled from the SOW.

### One-pager
- What single thing is this for — an offer, an industry, or the firm?
- Where will it be used — email attachment, leave-behind, partner share, conference handout?

### Internal doc
- What kind — meeting notes, retro, decision memo, planning doc?
- Audience — the co-founders, or extended (advisor, partner)?
- Action items vs reference? If it records a decision, apply `decision-governance` (proposed vs decided).

## When the teammate gives you a transcript

Read it once. Pull:
- Decision-maker name + role + what they kept coming back to
- Specific pain language they used (quote it back in the doc)
- Tenant, licensing and systems mentioned — even partial
- Size and industry
- Compliance obligations mentioned (CMMC, DFARS, ITAR, HIPAA)
- Any explicit asks ("send us a proposal by Friday")
- Any deal-breakers (budget, timeline, must-haves, data-handling concerns)

Then ask only what's *not* in the transcript.

## When the teammate gives you a prior doc to remix

Read it. Note what's reusable (structure, voice, sections) and what needs to change (client, industry, scope, price, dates, outcomes). If its positioning, offers, team or pricing don't match current canon, flag what's out of date and rewrite to canon; take any price from the teammate, never from the old doc. Confirm the new client and scope, then write.
